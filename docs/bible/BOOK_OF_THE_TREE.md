## Genesis

### The Book of the Trunk

*etiology*

This book answers one question: why there is a tree at all, rather than a folder of scripts. It is not a history of the code; it is an account of a decision that made later decisions possible.

Read it for the shape of the claim. The trunk is canon, engine and instruments; the branches are the packages. A branch is a package only when it can stand alone, which is a claim about gates and not about file layout.

#### Genesis 1

##### The trunk

**1** In the beginning there was one tree, and the tree had a trunk, and the trunk held the canon.

**2** And the trunk put forth branches: the forensic, and the behavioural, and the instruments that grade them. *(see genesis 1:1)*

> **editorial** — The three are not a hierarchy. The instruments are named beside the branches because a package that grades nothing is a folder with a README.

##### The branches

**3** And the branches were one repository, and no branch was called a package until it could stand alone. *(see genesis 1:2)*

> **cross** — Standing alone means a manifest, a gate and a published tree, in that order; see the packaging plan.

**4** And the name of the tree was Azazael, and the name was not a product, nor a promise, nor a client. *(see genesis 1:1)*

## Leviticus

### The Book of the Gates

*law*

This book is the law code, and every statute in it is bound to the check that executes it. That binding is the point of the book: a commandment with nothing running behind it teaches a reader to expect enforcement where there is none.

The statutes here are not new law. Each cites a rule already in the canon and points at the instrument that proves it is kept; where the two disagree, the canon governs and this book is wrong.

The book is not a confession and the reader is not asked to be edified. It exists so that the next instrument of the same shape is recognised before it reports.

## Glossary

- **trunk** — the canon, the engine and the instruments that every package grows out of. (see genesis 1:1)
- **apparatus** — the variants and notes that record what a reading used to say, and why it stopped saying it. (see the_falls 1:1)
- **statute** — a law bound to the check that executes it; a law with no check is a proverb and is filed as wisdom. (see leviticus 1:2)
- **practice** — the instruction attached to a verse: the symptom, its cause, the command that detects it, the remedy, and the harvested records that support it. (see sentinel 1:1)
- **domain book** — a book whose subject is one declared domain, so that the guide is organised by the trees it describes rather than by a heading someone liked. (see hives 1:1)
- **harvest** — the ledger of records extracted from every environment's error log, lessons, memories and decisions, regenerated by a command. (see sentinel 1:2)
